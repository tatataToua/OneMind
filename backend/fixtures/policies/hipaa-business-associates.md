# Business Associate Agreements (BAA)

**Source:** 45 CFR 164.502(e), 164.504(e) — paraphrased for internal guidance.
**Owner:** Privacy Office | **Last reviewed:** 2026-04-11

A covered entity may disclose protected health information (PHI) to a business
associate only after obtaining satisfactory assurances, documented in a written
Business Associate Agreement, that the associate will safeguard the information.

## When a BAA is required

A BAA is required when a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI
on our behalf. This includes cloud hosting providers with access to PHI at rest,
even where that access is nominally incidental.

## When a BAA is NOT required

A BAA is **not** required where the vendor receives only de-identified data that
meets the standard in 45 CFR 164.514(b). De-identified data is not PHI, and the
Privacy Rule does not restrict its use or disclosure.

Two caveats apply. First, the de-identification must be complete before the data
leaves our control — a vendor cannot de-identify on our behalf without a BAA,
because it necessarily handles PHI in order to do so. Second, any
re-identification key must be retained by us and never disclosed to the vendor.

A conduit exception also applies to entities that merely transport data without
accessing it other than randomly or incidentally, such as a postal service or an
ISP. Cloud storage providers do **not** qualify for this exception.
